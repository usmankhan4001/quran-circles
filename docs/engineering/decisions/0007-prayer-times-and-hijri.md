# 0007: Prayer times and the Hijri date, calculated on the phone

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-10-16
Lane: content

## Context

V1 asked the online Aladhan service for prayer times (method 1, school 1) and for the Hijri date. V2 must show the next prayer, a countdown and a week or month calendar with no connection, and must let the admin move the Hijri date by -2 to +2 days (`hijriOffsetDays` in `config.get`, meaning "days to add to the calculated Hijri date"). So both are calculated on the device.

## Options

- Keep calling Aladhan and cache the answers. Needs a connection the first time, and a request per day and city.
- Port the PrayTimes.org formulas ourselves. Full control, but we would own astronomy code and its edge cases (high latitudes, rounding).
- Use the `adhan` library for prayer times; use a small vendored Umm al-Qura table for the Hijri date.

## Decision

**Prayer times: `adhan` 4.4.6** (MIT, no dependencies, last published 2026-08-31, pinned exactly). Settings are the ones V1 sent to Aladhan:

- Method: University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi (`CalculationMethod.Karachi()`: Fajr 18 degrees, Isha 18 degrees).
- Asr: Hanafi (shadow twice the object's length), as V1 sent `school=1`.
- Rounding: nearest minute (the library default).
- `methodAdjustments.dhuhr` set to 0. `adhan` adds a one minute margin to Dhuhr for this method; Aladhan does not. The test `leaves out the one minute margin` pins this. If a live comparison shows Aladhan does add it, flip the one line in `karachiHanafi()` in `src/prayer/calc.ts`.
- The day is the calendar day at the place (Pakistan Standard Time, fixed UTC+5, no daylight saving), never the phone's time zone. Results do not change with the phone's zone (tested).

**Hijri date: the Umm al-Qura calendar from a vendored table plus the admin offset.** `src/prayer/umalqura-data.ts` holds, for each Hijri year 1318 to 1500 (1900 to 2077), the day the year starts and a bitmask of its 30-day months. It is generated by `scripts/gen-umalqura.mjs` from `@umalqura/core` 0.0.7 (MIT, Alaa Masoud; attribution in the file header), which stays a dev dependency only. The library itself was not used at run time because it depends on the phone's time zone. The admin offset is added to the civil day before the lookup, so +1 moves the date forward one day and the rules for month lengths stay intact. Offsets outside -2..2 are clamped.

Pakistan starts months by moon sighting, usually a day after Saudi Arabia. The offset is how the admin keeps the shown date in line with the national announcement. The screen says so in words (`prayer.hijriNote`).

The Hijri date changes at midnight at the place. Islamic days begin at Maghrib; showing the next day's date after Maghrib is possible later and is a product question, not a calculation one.

## How it was checked

- Live Aladhan could not be reached from the build environment. Instead `src/test/praytimesReference.ts` is an independent implementation of the published PrayTimes.org algorithm (the one Aladhan documents for the Karachi method), written from its formulas. 6 cities x 7 dates across the year (January, both equinoxes, both solstices, 16 October, February) agree with `adhan` within one minute (42 cases). Two sets of times are pinned (Karachi 2026-10-16, Lahore 2026-06-21).
- `packages/content/scripts/compare-aladhan.mjs` compares the same configuration with the live service for six cities and five dates and exits non-zero on a difference over one minute. **Run it once with internet access and note the date and result here.** Not yet run: _pending_.
- Hijri: the vendored table is compared with `@umalqura/core` for every day from 1900 to 2077; offsets -2 to +2 are tested, including crossing a month and a year end; the answer is the same with `TZ` set to UTC, Karachi, Los Angeles and Auckland.

## Consequences

- Prayer times and the Hijri date work offline and cost no requests.
- A difference of one minute from other apps is normal (different rounding or margin conventions).
- The Hijri table ends at 2077. After that `hijriDate` returns null and the screens leave the date out.
- Calculated times are shown in Pakistan Standard Time. A place outside Pakistan can pass `utcOffsetMinutes`; the city list only has Pakistani cities for now.
- Revisit: the Dhuhr margin after the live comparison; whether the Hijri day should turn over at Maghrib.
